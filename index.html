Race Ahead

A driving harness that rewrites itself between laps, from its own laps only.

Race the champion · play Watch the video · 2:30

01 · The starting point

A hand-written harness, and a slow lap

A language model, Jev, drives the kart through a harness: before every section of the track it answers a few questions in natural language, and the harness turns the answers into steering and throttle.

We start from deliberately crude harnesses: two questions, no rules, slow fixed target speeds. On four tracks the system never trained on, they need far longer than the game's own AI, our oracle.

02 · The system contribution 1 · two roles

One model drives, another one rewrites the harness

One lap runs along the top straight; one round of evolution goes once around the circuit. Hover over (or tap) any stop for details.

    03 · Evolution on track

    Same track, six laps: how the champion got faster

    The champion of one run (crude start 2, seed 1) at five points of its 40 rounds, and the oracle, on the training track zengarden. Each lap is replayed in the game from the answers Jev gave in the recorded lap; the replayed lap times match the records.

    Round 0 never gets far above 16 m/s. Round 7 raises the target speeds, round 11 merges a branch that sets the speed from the road's curvature, round 14 lets the kart run flat out on straights (its dip to almost zero is a rescue after leaving the road, part of the recorded lap); later rounds refine steering and braking, and the final champion finishes without a rescue.

    04 · Inside a round contribution 2 · evidence and written verdicts

    Every change is measured three ways, then judged in writing

    Each square is one round: the system's total time on 10 practice tracks after that round (lower is faster). Click a square to see, in plain words, what the system tried, how each change did on the track, and why it was kept or thrown away. Round 15 is open: one change kept, one rejected.

    05 · Replayable history contribution 3

    Big redesigns, crashes and repairs stay in the record

    The cyan line is the best version so far; each square on it is a round that made it faster (the number is its total time in seconds). A big redesign is built on a side track below the line and joins the main line only once it is faster and the reviewer agrees. A red cross is a round where the new version crashed. In rounds 1–5 a bold rewrite crashed on every track; each error message went back to the system until it gave the redesign up. In round 20 another redesign crashed on every track, was repaired in round 21, and joined the main line. Click any side track.
